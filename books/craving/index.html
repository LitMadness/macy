“Put me down for two dozen of those pies,” he says, standing so abruptly his chair bumps into the table behind him. “And a mix of everything else. Keep four of the pies separate and combine twenty of them with these cupcakes and cookies, a dozen or so with each of the pies.”

“So twenty packages with one pie and an assortment of a dozen cookies and cupcakes, and one package with four pies.” He nods and I stand too, aware of how he towers over me. “That’s a big order.”

“It’s been a good year at the yard. They deserve it.”

I scramble over to the counter and grab a business card from the holder that looks like an ice cream sundae. “Here’s our email. Let me know the delivery date when you can. At least two or three business days’ notice will work. More would be super.”

He looks at the card but doesn’t take it. “What if I’d rather call?”

“Not a problem.” AT&T just canceled everyone’s landlines, despite decades of relying on it and many people just outside of town having zero cell reception because the towers get blocked by the mountains. Daphne’s still figuring out our options, hoping to keep the same number with a Wi-Fi service, but we aren’t sure yet. So I scribble my personal number on the back and hold it out. “Here you go.”

“That works.” It’s a low growl as his fingers brush mine—intentionally?—as he takes the card.

“I’ll call you,” he says with a confident nod, tucking the card into his shirt pocket, right over his heart.

I walk him to the door, unlocking it and pulling it open. The cold December air rushes in, breaking the warm spell of the bakery. Feels as though a front has blown through just since he arrived.

That or his presence has got you all hot and bothered.

“Good night, Mr. Tanner,” I say, hoping I sound professional. Especially after the anything-but-professional thoughts I’ve had since he arrived.

He pauses crossing the threshold, silhouetted against the dark night. “Royce,” he corrects. “Just Royce from now on.”

“Good night, Royce. Thanks for stopping by.”

He nods once and his eyes seem to linger on my face before he steps outside. I watch him stride to his truck, his broad shoulders squared against the cold, and I realize I’ve been holding my breath.

He starts his engine, but refuses to put it in reverse until I close and lock the door. I do, watching as his taillights disappear down the single paved street in Wild Rose. My heart’s racing with thoughts I have no business thinking about a much older man.

Older man? That’s not the problem, and you know it.

Yeah, Dad was twelve years older than Mom. I’ve always appreciated a good mature man, I just haven’t reacted to any. Until now.

My ex-boyfriend’s father.

Naughty, naughty girl.

Maybe so. And yet, I can’t stop wondering what his beard would feel like against my skin. His lips against my mouth. His cherry-pie-coated tongue…

Well, everywhere.

I have a feeling it’s going to be a long, lonely night.
