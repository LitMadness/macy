Outlaws Grump Worth Scoring    

Former pro-hockey enforcer. Grumpy single dad. Motorcycle-riding pain in the ass. And if I want him, mine?

I went to the bar to support my best friend and the Austin Outlaws’ new team colors.

I didn’t expect to end up arguing with a big bearded stranger who thinks my teal-and-plum uniforms look like Easter Bunny puke. (What a jerk!)

I really didn’t expect to want him. This man whose scowl and sheer size are as intimidating as his grizzled nickname.

A little older, a lot growlier, and about as far from my usual type as a man can get, Lyle manages to both captivate and irritate. But underneath the attitude is a devoted dad, a fiercely protective streak he doesn’t try to hide, and a man who makes me feel safer after one night than my lying ex managed in months.

There’s just one teeny-tiny problem.

My random sexy biker isn’t random at all.

He's Lyle Bearden—former professional player, now co-owner of the Austin Outlaws and the “hockey hard-ass” my best friend (the team’s other owner) has been complaining about for months.

I should run. Get far away and fast.

Instead of running, I’m standing my ground. Glorying in our sparks, in my back against the wall, in his lips on mine. Starting to wonder if the biggest risk I’ve taken on a man in years might finally be the one that pays off.

I slept with the enemy. And I can’t wait to do it again.

Ack! I’m a goner.

“What about them?” She crosses one booted leg over the other, angles down to pull some imaginary lint off the embroidered leather near the top of the shaft.

The movement gives me a glimpse of cleavage beneath the scooped front of her black dress, and I drag my eyes back up to her face. She catches me looking, and she’s not impressed.

“What? I’m not supposed to look?” I say, trying to regain my footing. Have I read her wrong? “To admire?”

“You’re not supposed to pretend like you’re not.”

“So obvious admiration and leering is approved?” Just to make sure, I dip my chin and stare straight down her dress. Can’t see anything from this angle, but we’re still learning each other, and yeah, I’m curious how far I can push.

“In your case, I’ll allow it.” She starts by smiling and then she’s laughing outright. “It was the leering that did it.”

Good. She’s got a sense of humor. And she gets mine, too. “About those uniforms…” I swing back to what brought us together tonight. “Do you really think purple and turquoise scream ‘aggressive hockey team’?”

“Teal and plum, you mean?” Yeah, I’d screwed them up on purpose that time. With another toss of that bracelet, she finishes, “I think they scream ‘memorable,’ which is exactly what an expansion team needs.” She turns on her stool to fully face me. “But you know what? I don’t need to defend my work to some random grump in a bar.”

“Then why are you still sitting here?”

Her smile widens. “Maybe I like talking about rides and arguing with difficult men.”

No, I didn’t read her wrong at all.
